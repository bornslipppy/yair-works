AI Gateway Wonderful

Every enterprise now runs a dozen AI tools, each with its own keys, bills and blind spots. Wonderful AI Gateway is the one layer every request passes through — where an organisation decides who may use which model, how much, and for what.

The Problem

AI usage was outpacing AI control. No one could say which team spent most, what was being pasted into prompts, or who accessed what. A dashboard would only describe the damage afterwards; a gateway sits in the path of every request. So I designed it as an enforcement point, not a report — policy authored once, applied before a request leaves the building.

The Decisive Choices

Guardrails fail closed, with a graded response. A control that only knows “block” gets switched off, so every policy pairs a detector with a consequence: block, warn or reroute.

A cap is a ceiling, not an allocation. A $3,000 model cap under a $5,000 limit reserves nothing. The rule editor makes that consequence legible before save.

Degrade, don’t break. At a limit or an outage, requests move to a cheaper or fallback model instead of stopping work.

How I Worked

No static mockups. I built the product with Claude Code as a working prototype across eight surfaces, held to its briefs by 57 automated tests — including 5,110 reconciliations that check every filtered total still adds up. Try the prototype.

The Outcome

Wonderful runs on its own gateway with 100% internal adoption, which led to a successful customer pilot and signed contracts. It’s live as part of the Wonderful AI OS.

What I left out mattered as much: no shared group wallets, reserved budget slices or throttling. Each was requested, and each made policy harder to predict. A safeguard people can’t predict is one they route around.
