AI Gateway Wonderful

Every enterprise now runs on a dozen AI tools — coding agents, copilots, internal agents — each with its own keys, bills and blind spots. Wonderful AI Gateway is the single layer every one of those requests passes through on its way to Anthropic, OpenAI, Google and the rest.

As Principal Product Designer, I took it from first brief to a working, tested prototype built with Claude Code: the place where an organisation decides who may use which model, how much, for what — and what happens when a request crosses the line.

The Problem

AI usage was outpacing AI control. Teams adopted tools independently, so nobody could answer basic questions: which team spent the most last month, what employees were pasting into prompts, whether we could prove who accessed what if an auditor asked. A spend dashboard would only have described the damage after it happened. A gateway sits in the path of every request — that makes it an enforcement point, not a report, and it changed what the product had to be.

The Reframe

I organised the product around one object: the request. Every surface answers a question about it — who sent it, whether it was allowed, whether it was safe, whether it was affordable, and where it should go. Policy is authored once and enforced before the request leaves the perimeter; analytics exist to explain enforcement, not to replace it.

The Decisive Choices

Guardrails that fail closed, with a graded response. A control that only knows “block” gets switched off the first time it blocks the CFO, so every policy pairs a detector with a consequence — block, warn, or reroute — and Wonderful’s defaults ship on, beside the admin’s own.

A cap is a ceiling, not an allocation. The most consequential misreading in the product: a $3,000 cap on premium models under a $5,000 limit does not reserve the other $2,000. The rule editor, its summary and its validation make the consequence of a rule legible before save.

Degrade, don’t break. Hitting a limit or a provider outage shouldn’t stop work mid-task: rules can move requests to a lower-cost model, and failover chains reroute when a provider goes down. Safety and continuity stop being a trade-off.

How I Worked

No static mockups. I built the product with Claude Code as a working front-end prototype across eight surfaces, so engineering, sales and customers reacted to real behaviour — filters that compose, rules that validate, limits that expire. Briefs were written as outcomes and constraints rather than screens, and the prototype was held to them by 57 automated tests, including 5,110 accounting reconciliations that check every filtered total still adds up. Try the prototype.

What I Didn’t Build

No shared group wallets — limits govern each person, so one heavy user can’t drain a team. No reserved budget slices. No throttling, which hid limits instead of explaining them. And no alert that flags spend in isolation: two engineers can each spend $27K in a month, one shipping an internal CRM and the other nothing. Each was requested; each would have made policy harder to predict. A safeguard people can’t predict is a safeguard they route around.

The Outcome

Wonderful runs on its own gateway — dogfooded internally with 100% adoption, which made us its first and harshest customer. From there, a successful customer pilot and signed contracts with prospective customers. Wonderful AI Gateway is live as part of the Wonderful AI OS.
